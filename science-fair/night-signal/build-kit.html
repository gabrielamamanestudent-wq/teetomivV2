No soldering for you. Everything plugs together with jumper wires, and a USB cable powers it all night. The one exception is 4 pins on the oxygen sensor: a teacher or anyone with a soldering iron can do them in about 5 minutes. Getting help with one build step is normal at a science fair, so thank them in your acknowledgements.

The wiring at a glance

3V3VIN (5 V)GNDSDASCL

Each sensor has its own four pins, so nothing is shared. The pin names are printed on the ESP32: match the names, not the positions. A little bump in a wire means it passes over the other wire without touching it. Swipe sideways to see the whole diagram.

Step 1 · Get 4 pins soldered on the oxygen sensor 5 min · helper

  1. Give your helper the MAX30102 board and its strip of pins. Ask them to solder pins to at least VIN, SDA, SCL and GND (doing all 7 is fine).
  2. The pins must stick out of the back, which is the side without the small black sensor window. The ESP32 and the GY-521 already come with pins.

Step 2 · Install the software 15 min · one time

  1. Install the Arduino IDE from arduino.cc/en/software.
  2. Go to Arduino IDE ▸ Settings ▸ Additional boards manager URLs and paste https://espressif.github.io/arduino-esp32/package_esp32_index.json. Then open Tools ▸ Board ▸ Boards Manager and install esp32 by Espressif Systems.
  3. Open Sketch ▸ Include Library ▸ Manage Libraries and install SparkFun MAX3010x Pulse and Proximity Sensor Library.

Step 3 · Plug in the wires 15 min

Oxygen sensor (MAX30102), on the right side

These wires go to your finger, so make each one about 40 cm by joining two wires end to end:

  1. Push a male–female wire's female end onto the ESP32 pin.
  2. Push its male end into a female–female wire.
  3. Push the female–female wire's other end onto the sensor.

Leave the GY-521's XDA, XCL, AD0 and INT pins empty. Push every plug all the way on, and wrap a little tape around each place where two wires join so they can't pull apart.

Step 4 · Upload the code 10 min

  1. Connect the ESP32 to your MacBook with the 3 m cable plus the USB-C adapter.
  2. Open 4 - Code/firmware/night_signal_esp32.ino. Choose Tools ▸ Board ▸ esp32 ▸ ESP32 Dev Module, then Tools ▸ Port ▸ /dev/cu.usbserial-…. If no port appears, install the Silicon Labs CP210x driver and try again.
  3. Click Upload (the → arrow). If it sticks on Connecting...., hold the BOOT button on the ESP32 until it starts.
  4. Open Tools ▸ Serial Monitor at 115200. You should see #STATUS,fw=3.1,max30102=OK,mpu6050=OK,battery=-1. battery=-1 just means it's on USB power.
  5. Rest a fingertip on the sensor. After about 15 s, numbers like 372145,68,1,97,1,… appear. Two OKs means the electronics work.

Step 5 · Make the finger cuff 10 min · scissors

  1. Cut ≈ 80 mm of the VELCRO roll, then cut a small 1 cm slit in the middle.
  2. Hold the sensor on the soft side with its black window facing up, and push its plugs through the slit. If the plugs are already on the sensor's pins, unplug them, push the bare pins through the slit, then plug them back in.
  3. Wrap it around the pad of your index finger with the window touching the skin. It should be snug, not tight, because a tight cuff squeezes the blood out and the readings get worse.
  4. Wrap one layer of black electrical tape around the outside to block room light.

Step 6 · Put it on 5 min

  1. Put the ESP32 in the armband pouch and wear it on your forearm. Let the finger wires and the USB cable come out through the zip.
  2. Plug the 3 m cable into a USB phone charger next to the bed. That powers it all night.
Sleep-position nights: motion sensor on the chest. position.py expects the GY-521 flat on your chest (breastbone), with its chips facing out and the printed X arrow pointing to your right side.
  1. Lengthen its 4 wires to about 60 cm: plug two male–female wires into each other, then a female–female wire on the end.
  2. Tape the board to your chest with medical tape. No code change is needed.
  3. Check it: lie on your back, left side, right side and stomach for 1 minute each, then run position.py. It should name all four positions correctly.
For demos and the oxygen tests, it can lie flat in the pouch.

Step 7 · Connect to your Mac by Bluetooth

The ESP32 appears as a Bluetooth device named NightSignal. Your computer treats it like a cable connection, so the scripts and the app read it the same way.

  1. Turn it on. Plug the USB cable into the charger. The blue LED blinks once per second when it is working. Fast blinking means a sensor isn't plugged in properly.
  2. Pair it. Open System Settings ▸ Bluetooth, find NightSignal under Nearby Devices and click Connect. If it asks for a PIN, try 1234.
  3. Find its name. Open Terminal and type ls /dev/cu.*, then look for /dev/cu.NightSignal.
  4. Check that the device works. It must say READY. 
    cd "4 - Code/analysis"
pip3 install -r requirements.txt
python3 check_device.py --port /dev/cu.NightSignal
  5. Record a night. caffeinate stops the Mac from sleeping. Press Ctrl+C in the morning. 
    caffeinate -i python3 record.py --port /dev/cu.NightSignal
  6. Watch it live in the app.
    1. In the 3 - App folder, run python3 start_app.py.
    2. Open Chrome at http://localhost:8000 and go to the Live tab.
    3. Click Connect device and pick NightSignal.

In Bluetooth settings the Mac often shows NightSignal as "Not connected". That is normal. The link opens when a program opens the port.

Windows

  1. Open Settings ▸ Bluetooth & devices ▸ Add device ▸ Bluetooth and choose NightSignal.
  2. Open More Bluetooth settings ▸ COM Ports. The Outgoing port (for example COM5) is the one to use.
  3. Run python check_device.py --port COM5, then python record.py --port COM5.
If Bluetooth won't cooperate:
  1. Forget This Device, unplug the ESP32 and plug it back in, then pair it again.
  2. Some macOS versions are fussy with ESP32 Bluetooth. The USB cable always works: plug the 3 m cable into the Mac (with the adapter) instead of the charger, and use the same commands with the /dev/cu.usbserial-… port. The data is identical.

Troubleshooting

SymptomFix
max30102=FAILA plug is loose, or SDA/SCL are swapped. VIN must go to 3V3. Some purple MAX30102 boards need a small fix: send a photo and we'll sort it out.
mpu6050=FAILA plug is loose, or SDA/SCL are swapped (SDA→D33, SCL→D32). VCC goes to VIN.
finger=0 the whole timeThe cuff is loose or the sensor window is covered. A cold finger can also cause it, so warm your hands.
Readings jump aroundMovement, a cuff that's too tight, or room light reaching the sensor. Add black tape.
No port in Tools ▸ PortUse the Anker cable (some cables only charge). Install the CP210x driver.
Upload stuck on Connecting...Hold BOOT while it uploads.
Data stops in the nightA plug came loose or the Mac slept. Tape the joins, and use caffeinate.
